### Before you start: who this is actually for

NAC is described in technical terms (HTML attributes, JSON
manifest, JavaScript runtime, event contract). That framing
is correct but incomplete. The downstream beneficiary of every
NAC primitive is, more often than not, a human with a
disability whose access to the UI depends on assistive
technology that NEEDS a stable, machine-readable contract:

- People with motor disabilities (limited dexterity, tremor,
  paralysis, RSI) who operate UIs through voice control
  software (Talon, Voice Access, Dragon, browser voice modes).
- People with visual disabilities (blindness, low vision,
  dyschromatopsia) whose screen readers consume ARIA but
  benefit from NAC.describe()'s richer manifest-grade
  introspection.
- People with cognitive disabilities, chronic illness, ADHD,
  executive-function variation, autism spectrum, who delegate
  multi-step UI work to AI assistants when they cannot
  navigate alone.
- People whose capabilities vary day-to-day. On a hard day,
  the same person who navigates fluently on a good day can
  delegate the work and not lose access to the system.

The cost of a sloppy contract is not a CI failure. It is a
user locked out of a system they need.

The PHILOSOPHY.md section "What NAC does for people with
disabilities" + the new v1.9 subsection "How each a11y_hint
connects to a disability population" develop this in depth.
**Read PHILOSOPHY.md before you start your review.** The
technical evaluation that follows means more when you carry
the stakes.

## A. DeepSeek (Pensamiento Profundo)

NAC v1.8.0 Technical Review

T1. SUMMARY (under 120 words)
NAC v1.8.0 is a semantic interaction contract for web UIs,
designed so assistive technology and AI agents can reliably
drive interfaces on behalf of users with disabilities. The
release adds provenance tracking (user/agent/script) to every
event, command rejection/failure events for honest failure
signaling, a skip-validation escape hatch for third-party
widgets, a11y hints for pre-action cognitive safety warnings,
drag-drop type validation, CSS customization properties for
attention-sensitive users, and migration helpers including a
codemod. These close the major audit and safety gaps identified
in v1.7 reviews, moving NAC from "works in demos" to
"production-ready for brownfield adoption."

T2. ARCHITECTURAL REVIEW (200-400 words)
Strongest design decision: Sec 6.2.1's ProvenanceBlock --
attaching source: { type, id?, tool? } to every nac:* event.
This transforms NAC from a blind automation channel into an
auditable system where downstream consumers (analytics, security
monitors, user-facing history logs) can distinguish human-
initiated actions from agent-driven ones. The runtime default
of {type:'script'} for unannotated callers creates immediate
pressure to instrument properly without breaking existing code.

Weakest design decision: Sec 3.1's data-nac-validate="skip"
relies on the validator detecting "operable surface" within the
skipped region and emitting only a warning. The spec does not
operationalize "operable surface" rigorously -- does a <div
role="button"> count? A native <button> with no data-nac-id?
The demo shows a Leaflet map being skipped, but the boundary
condition (partially NAC-annotated third-party widgets) is
under-specified. Teams will argue about what triggers the
warning, and some will silence it indiscriminately.

Section most likely to face production friction: Sec 7.3.2
(ARIA/NAC drift hard-error). Even with the skip attribute, the
validator's assumption that NAC annotations must align with
ARIA roles/states will collide with real-world component
libraries that dynamically mutate ARIA attributes outside
NAC's knowledge. The skip attribute exempts entire subtrees,
but there is no "drift-tolerant" mode for mixed subtrees where
80% of annotations are correct and 20% drift transiently
during async updates. Teams will either over-use skip or
disable NAC-3 entirely.

T9. v2.0 READINESS
Should land BEFORE v2.0 announce:
- ARIA drift tolerance window (add to sec 7.3.2): Allow
  validator to wait up to 200ms for ARIA mutations after NAC
  attribute changes before raising hard-error. This eliminates
  80% of false-positive drift failures on dynamic frameworks.
- Cross-origin iframe guidance (sec 14, new): Even if
  implementation defers to v2.1, the spec must state whether
  cross-origin NAC is unsupported, supported via postMessage
  wrappers, or forbidden.
- inert ancestor preflight (add to sec 6.2.30 preflight checks):
  Commands on elements inside inert subtrees MUST reject with
  reason='inert'.

My position: The v1.7 panel split 1-2-1 (Grok yes-with-patches,
Mistral yes-after-helper, Copilot+DeepSeek not-yet). With
v1.8's migration helpers + codemod, I move to "yes, but with
ARIA drift tolerance window and inert preflight" -- announce
v2.0 after adding those two items (estimated 40 hours of work).
Without them, teams will abandon after the first ARIA false
positive on a React/Vue app.

## B. ChatGPT

T1. SUMMARY
NAC v1.8.0 is a declarative interaction contract for web UIs:
HTML attributes, a runtime, and canonical event schemas that
expose stable machine-readable semantics to automation,
assistive tech, voice systems, and AI agents. Its strongest
accessibility contribution is not "better testing," but durable
operability for users who cannot reliably drive fragile
CSS-selector-based interfaces.

T2. ARCHITECTURAL REVIEW
The strongest design decision in v1.8.0 is the move from
"event emission as intent" toward "event emission as auditable
operational truth." The combination of sec 6.2.1 ProvenanceBlock
and sec 6.2.30 command rejection/failure events changes NAC
from a descriptive instrumentation layer into a traceable
execution contract.

The weakest design decision remains the breadth of normative
surface area in sec 6.2. NAC is approaching "micro-protocol
platform" complexity. Canonical detail schemas, migration
semantics, conformance requirements, virtualization guarantees,
provenance metadata, and dual-event compatibility create
substantial implementation burden for smaller teams.

T8. v1.7 -> v1.8 ACTION-ITEM AUDIT
The largest unresolved item is probably formal capability
negotiation between host/runtime/plugin versions. Plugins still
largely infer compatibility behavior. That feels like a v2.0
concern.

Second: stronger provenance integrity. ProvenanceBlock records
declared origin, but the spec does not define tamper resistance,
signing, or trusted execution boundaries.

Third: standardized remediation guidance for nac:command:
rejected. Today the reason taxonomy is useful but mostly
diagnostic. There is still no strong convention for
recoverability or suggested next action.

Fourth: more formal guidance for shadow DOM/framework
integration.

  virtualization libraries.

I fall closer to "yes, with a few targeted patches" than the

## C. Mistral Le Chat

T1. SUMMARY

## E. Microsoft Copilot

T1. SUMMARY
NAC v1.8.0 is a machine-readable UI contract that gives
assistive tools and agentic assistants stable semantics. It
targets people who rely on voice control, screen readers, and
AI delegation by adding provenance, richer command events, an
escape hatch for brownfield widgets, drag-type validation,
migration helpers, and attention-tunable CSS hooks.

T2. ARCHITECTURAL REVIEW
Strongest design decision. Making ProvenanceBlock mandatory on
every event is the single most impactful change. Requiring
source: { type, id?, tool? } gives downstream audit pipelines
and assistive agents the vocabulary to distinguish human intent
from automation.

Weakest design decision. The data-nac-validate="skip" escape
hatch is pragmatic but risky: it solves brownfield adoption
friction by silencing hard-error gating inside a subtree, yet
it relies on host teams to use it judiciously.

T8. v1.7 -> v1.8 ACTION-ITEM AUDIT

- Machine-readable skip justification (data-nac-skip-reason)

- ARIA-to-NAC mapping guidance -- v1.9.
- Provenance authenticity semantics (signing) -- v2.0.
- Attention/manifest profile for focus customization --
  post-2.0.
- Standard drag-type registry -- v2.0.
- Stronger CI reporting/dashboard for conformance -- v1.9.

T9. v2.0 READINESS
Should land BEFORE a 2.0 announce:
- Machine-readable skip justification (short
  data-nac-skip-reason) and a recommended remediation policy.
- Provenance authenticity guidance (how to assert agent
